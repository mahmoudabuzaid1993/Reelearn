# Learning science and feed mechanics behind short-form vertical video for workplace learning

Research date: 2026-10-29. Evidence labels used throughout: **[PR]** peer-reviewed; **[PP]** preprint / thesis / not yet peer-reviewed; **[VENDOR]** vendor or marketing source; **[PRACT]** practitioner opinion; **[CLASSIC]** pre-2020 foundational study; **[AGG]** aggregator / secondary statistic whose primary source could not be verified.

## KQ1. Does short-form video produce learning and retention? What does 2020–2026 research say about attention span and "TikTok brain", and what do microlearning meta-analyses show?

### Takeaway
Two bodies of evidence point in opposite directions and must be kept separate: (1) *microlearning* (short, focused units, usually with quizzes and spacing) reliably beats long-form delivery on immediate post-tests in small studies, but the meta-analytic base is thin (5–12 studies, mostly higher-ed or EFL, few delayed-retention measures, no workplace meta-analysis found); (2) *heavy or compulsive consumption of short-form video feeds* is consistently, moderately correlated with worse attention and inhibitory control (r ≈ −0.34 to −0.41 across 70 studies), but the evidence is cross-sectional, self-reported, and driven by addiction-scale measures rather than minutes of use, so "TikTok brain" as a causal claim is not established.

### Cited Findings

**Short-form video consumption and cognition ("TikTok brain")**
- Nguyen et al. (2025), *Psychological Bulletin* (doi 10.1037/bul0000498), "Feeds, Feelings, and Focus": systematic review of 71 studies and meta-analysis of 70, N = 98,299, youths and adults, mostly Asian samples. Heavier short-form video use showed a moderate negative association with cognition overall; attention and inhibitory control were the most consistently affected domains; small but significant negative correlations with anxiety, depression, stress; no significant overall association with body image/self-esteem. **[PR]** — [PsyPost summary](https://www.psypost.org/large-meta-analysis-links-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-to-poorer-cognitive-and-mental-health/); [NYU abstract listing](https://ifp.nyu.edu/2025/journal-article-abstracts/bul0000498/)
- Reported coefficients from the same meta-analysis: overall cognition r = −.34; attention r = −.38; inhibitory control r = −.41. The strongest links appeared when use was measured with addiction-style scales; negative cognitive effects were only very weakly associated with raw duration of use. **[PR, numbers via secondary press]** — [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/s-is-all-that-scrolling-really-dulling-your-attention-researchers-combed-71-studies-the-link-is-real-but-how-compulsively-you-scroll-matters-more-than-how-long/); [Reason coverage](https://reason.com/2025/12/31/study-short-form-video-isnt-rotting-your-brain)
- Limitations acknowledged by the authors: most studies cross-sectional (no causality); self-reported usage; only ~1 in 10 studies controlled for general social media use; reverse causation plausible (people with attention problems may be drawn to feeds). **[PR]** — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/large-meta-analysis-links-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-to-poorer-cognitive-and-mental-health/)
- A 2025 medRxiv systematic review of 17 studies found high-frequency short-form video use "consistently associated with attentional disruption, reduced executive functioning, and emotional dysregulation," while flagging reliance on cross-sectional designs, self-report, and inconsistent operationalisation of cognitive outcomes. **[PP]** — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.27.25334540v1.full)
- "Taming the endless scroll" (European Child & Adolescent Psychiatry, 2026): systematic review of 42 studies (2021–2025) linking *problematic* short-video use to inattention and boredom in youth; correlational. **[PR]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00787-026-03083-7)
- A 2026 *International Journal of Adolescence and Youth* systematic review reports a negative association between short-video addiction and attention span, and cites Ye et al. (2023) finding that excessive short-video use undermines students' attention, participation and perceived learning effectiveness. **[PR]** — [Taylor & Francis](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02673843.2026.2623337)
- Ye et al. (2023), *Healthcare* 11(7):985: among Chinese vocational college students, higher short-video addiction was associated with stronger learning-avoidance motivation and weaker learning commitment. **[PR]** — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2227-9032/11/7/985); companion 2022 *Frontiers in Public Health* paper found short-video addiction reduced intrinsic and extrinsic learning motivation and learning well-being — [DOAJ record](https://doaj.org/article/33fe2cf98dc742c2aaf390eff6d3b3ec)
- Cross-sectional survey of parents of Thai children aged 6–12 linked short-form video use to inattentive symptoms. **[PR]** — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12230358/)
- **Contradicting evidence:** a 2025 study of Saudi international-school students found no strong correlation between daily short-form video time and declines in attention or memory. **[PP/low-tier journal]** — [HRMARS](https://hrmars.com/papers_submitted/24752/the-impact-of-short-form-videos-on-some-cognitive-abilities-among-saudi-international-school-students.pdf); an Indonesian college sample found no correlation between short-video addiction and sustained attention but a positive link to academic procrastination. **[PR, low-tier]** — [IJASSH](https://journal.untar.ac.id/index.php/ijassh/article/view/29006/17293)
- A 2026 study of 180 children found swipe and recommendation features increased task switching without a clear decline in short-term or working memory. **[Secondary summary of an integrative review]** — [note.com review summary](https://note.com/jpss4691/n/n3959f2e4c977?hl=en)

**Attention-span claims**
- Gloria Mark (UC Irvine): average time on a single screen before switching fell from ~2.5 minutes (early 2000s) to 47 seconds averaged across studies from 2016–2020; a 2016 study of 40 information workers logged a median online-screen focus of 40 seconds. This measures screen-switching, not a clinical "attention span." **[PR, via university interview]** — [UC Newsroom](https://www.universityofcalifornia.edu/news/how-sharpen-your-attention-and-meet-your-goals-2024); [Silicon Canals](https://siliconcanals.com/t-screen-attention-47-seconds-gloria-mark/)
- The "8-second attention span, shorter than a goldfish" claim traces to a 2015 Microsoft Canada report, has no peer-reviewed support, and has been debunked by the BBC and King's College London; the origin of the 8-second figure inside the report is itself unclear. **[PRACT/journalism]** — [WARC "The goldfish myth"](https://www.warc.com/newsandopinion/opinion/the_goldfish_myth/2806); [Medium/Psynamic tracing](https://medium.com/psynamic/8-second-attention-spans-tracking-the-myth-that-just-wont-quit-614dd2cb9792)

**Microlearning efficacy (meta-analyses and reviews 2019–2026)**
- Senadheera et al. (2024), *Journal of Multidisciplinary and Translational Research* 9(1): systematic review of 12 studies (from 602 retrieved) and meta-analysis of 5 (RevMan 5.4) in higher education; microlearning students (n = 344) outperformed macro-learning students (n = 310), p = 0.03. A snippet of a later version reports a mean difference of 12.6 points on theory post-tests (95% CI 1.2–23.9); no standardised effect size located. **[PR, small-journal]** — [JMTR PDF](https://journals.kln.ac.lk/jmtr/media/attachments/2024/07/26/jmtr_24_22.pdf)
- EFL speaking-skills meta-analysis (2024, IJLTER): 10 studies, N = 743, microlearning vs lecture SMD = 1.43 (95% CI 1.10–1.76). Note a blog cites a different CI (1.27–1.59); not workplace. **[PR]** — [IJLTER](https://www.ijlter.org/index.php/ijlter/article/download/10006/pdf)
- Balasundaram, Mathew & Nair (2024), *Journal of Learning for Development* 11(1): post-test control-group study with first-year MBA students; microlearning group quiz mean 6.6 vs 5.6 for the regular module (p < .05); reaction score 4.22 vs 3.8; significant only on enjoyment and technology dimensions; immediate post-test only. **[PR]** — [ERIC full text](https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1423546.pdf)
- De Gagne et al. (2019) scoping review of microlearning in health professions education: 94% of included studies assessed learner reaction (Kirkpatrick L1), 82% knowledge/skill (L2), few measured behaviour change. **[PR, CLASSIC-adjacent]** — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6683654/)
- Bozarth (Learning Guild, April 2024) literature report notes the Taylor & Hung (2022) review covered only 13 studies and concludes microlearning's anticipated benefits appear realised in the research — a narrative conclusion, not a pooled effect. **[PRACT research report]** — [Learning Guild PDF](https://cedma-europe.org/newsletter%20articles/eLearning%20Guild/Microlearning%20-%20Current%20State%20and%20Future%20Implications%20(Apr%2024).pdf)
- A January 2026 Zenodo review concludes microlearning is valuable "when implemented thoughtfully within comprehensive learning ecosystems," with significant research gaps remaining. **[PP]** — [Zenodo](https://zenodo.org/records/18477453)
- Vendor-circulated claims (e.g., "up to 60% better retention," "45–80% less training time") appear without primary sources. **[VENDOR/AGG]** — [Headway blog](https://makeheadway.com/blog/microlearning-statistics/); [Engageli](https://www.engageli.com/blog/20-microlearning-statistics-in-2025)

**Underlying learning-science mechanisms the format can exploit**
- Retrieval practice: Yang et al. (2021) meta-analysis of 222 classroom studies, N = 48,478: quizzing improved achievement g = 0.499 (0.427 bias-corrected); vs restudy g = 0.330; vs nothing g = 0.610; not significantly better than elaborative strategies; I² ≈ 88%. Rowland (2014) lab meta-analysis g ≈ 0.50. **[PR]** — [NSF-hosted paper discussing both](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10518320); [secondary stat roundup](https://studycardsai.com/blog/active-recall-statistics); Agarwal et al. (2021) classroom-only review: 49 effect sizes, 57% medium or large — [retrievalpractice.org](https://www.retrievalpractice.org/review)
- Spacing: Cepeda et al. (2006), *Psychological Bulletin*: 839 assessments across 317 experiments in 184 articles; inter-study interval and retention interval jointly determine final retention. **[CLASSIC]** — [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/); Thalheimer's 2006 Work-Learning Research report "Spacing Learning Events Over Time" synthesises the same literature for practitioners — [PDF](https://Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2006-thalheimer.pdf)
- Ebbinghaus forgetting curve: Murre & Dros (2015, *PLoS ONE*) replicated the 1880 savings curve with a single participant over 70 hours of learning; curve shows a likely upward jump at the 24-hour point. The widely reproduced "we forget 70% in 24 hours" infographic numbers are not from this replication. **[PR, single-subject]** — [DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/1eb262a059254c17b017e217aee289d0)

**Direct studies of TikTok-style educational video**
- 2022 UK study: TikTok use in an introductory statistics course had a beneficial impact on learner performance and self-perceived engagement. **[PR, small]** — [GuildHE repository](https://nul.repository.guildhe.ac.uk/id/eprint/2410/)
- 2024 grade-8 static-electricity study randomly assigned 59 learners to two TikTok-video groups and a control; outcomes not reported in the abstract retrieved. **[PP conference]** — [CERN Indico](https://indico.cern.ch/event/1162407/contributions/5931907)
- 2025 quasi-experiment (65 Indonesian junior-high students): educational TikTok videos improved vocabulary, effect size r = 0.4; authors call for long-term retention follow-up. **[PP thesis]** — [Walisongo](https://eprints.walisongo.ac.id/id/eprint/30643/)
- "Using TikTok in Education: micro-learning or nano-learning?" (2021) frames sub-60-second clips as "nano-learning" units that must be assembled into micro-learning objectives. **[PR]** — [An-Najah](https://staff.najah.edu/en/publications/12503/)
- Carpenter et al. (2024), *Teaching and Teacher Education* 142:104530, "How and why educators use TikTok: Come for the fun, stay for the learning?" **[PR]** — [cited in UBC course PDF](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec523/files/2025/09/TikTok-Microlearning-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- The format itself (15–90 s vertical clips) is not what produces learning; retrieval, spacing and feedback are. Short-form video is a *delivery container* whose learning value depends almost entirely on whether those mechanisms are wired in.
- Because the cognitive harms correlate with compulsive-use scores rather than minutes, a workplace product that deliberately avoids compulsive-loop mechanics can plausibly capture the engagement benefits of short clips without importing the attention costs — but no study has tested this directly.
- Almost all microlearning evidence measures immediate post-tests and reactions (Kirkpatrick L1/L2); product teams should not claim long-term retention or behaviour change from microlearning literature alone.

### Gaps
- No 2023–2025 meta-analysis of microlearning specifically in *workplace* training was found; the "Sözmen" meta-analysis referenced in the brief could not be located (only a reference-list trace). The workplace effect-size claims circulating online trace to EFL/higher-ed studies or unsourced vendor blogs.
- No peer-reviewed study was found measuring *delayed* (≥1 week) retention from a swipe/autoplay vertical feed in adults.
- No longitudinal or experimental study establishes that feed consumption causally reduces sustained attention; all large-N evidence is correlational.
- The arXiv "Can't Stop" friction-intervention study (2607.15818) could not be fetched (file too large); its quantified results are unverified.

## KQ2. Which feed mechanics drive engagement (variable reward, autoplay, infinite scroll, swipe, personalisation, social proof, streaks, notifications), what is their effect on time-on-app and learning, and what harms are documented?

### Takeaway
The best causal evidence exists for social reward (likes) operating as reinforcement learning, for autoplay/recommendations reducing users' sense of agency, and for streaks and notification personalisation lifting retention at Duolingo; the evidence that these mechanics *improve learning* (as opposed to time-on-app) is essentially absent, and the documented harms cluster around compulsive use, procrastination, sleep and stress rather than measured knowledge loss.

### Cited Findings

**Variable reward / social proof**
- Lindström et al. (2021), *Nature Communications*: reinforcement-learning models fitted to >1 million posts from >4,000 users across platforms show people post more after receiving many likes and less after few; an online experiment (n = 176) confirmed social rewards causally shape posting behaviour; meaningful individual-difference profiles in social reward learning. **[PR]** — [VU record](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/a-computational-reward-learning-account-of-social-media-engagemen/); [UvA press release](https://uva.nl/en/content/news/press-releases/2021/02/we-post-so-much-on-social-media-because-we-learn-from-rewards.html)
- A 2026 *Information & Management* paper argues "addiction" mis-describes the endless-scroll pattern and proposes "compulsive scrolling," attributing it to content types and recommendation systems. **[PR]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0378720626000169)

**Autoplay, infinite scroll, swipe, recommendations**
- Lukoff et al. (CHI 2021), survey of 120 YouTube users plus 13 co-design sessions: autoplay and recommendations primarily undermine sense of agency; search and playlists support it; users with a specific intention prefer higher-agency interfaces. **[PR]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11778)
- A 2026 scoping review notes platforms' recommendation systems, autoplay, infinite scroll and rapid clip transitions may encourage prolonged, repetitive viewing. **[PR]** — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212657026000760)
- An integrative review (summarised secondarily) reports experiments adding friction to autoplay and infinite scroll changed compulsive usage and time perception, but "not yet enough evidence that these features directly cause lasting changes in sustained attention." **[Secondary]** — [note.com summary](https://note.com/jpss4691/n/n3959f2e4c977?hl=en)
- "Endless Scrolling" (2026, *Journal of Media Psychology* via SAGE), 549 US college students: lower self-control and habitual use predicted procrastinatory short-video use, which was associated with more stress and worse sleep. **[PR]** — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21522715251404199)
- "Lost in the Scroll" (2026) investigates mindless smartphone use among adolescents. **[PR]** — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/09731342251380968)
- "Can't Stop" (arXiv 2607.15818) tests gradual interventions against infinite scrolling on short-video platforms, moderated by context and traits. **[PP]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.15818)

**Streaks**
- Duolingo blog: >6 million people were on a streak of 7+ days at time of writing; learners reaching a 7-day streak are 3.6× more likely to complete their course (not claimed as causal); a streak-milestone animation raised 7-day new-user retention by +1.7%; allowing two equipped Streak Freezes raised daily active learners by +0.38% relative; losing a streak "can be demotivating and may deter learners from starting one"; the company cites loss aversion and "slack" research (Penn/UCLA) as rationale. **[VENDOR, first-party experiments]** — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/)
- Jackson Shuttleworth (Duolingo Group PM, Retention), Lenny's Podcast, Dec 2024: 600+ streak-related experiments; retention is most fragile in the first seven days; copy change from "continue" to "commit to my goal" "significantly" raised retention (unquantified). **[PRACT]** — [Lenny's Newsletter](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)
- Third-party summaries cite a Streak Wager test lifting retention ~14% (reported as Day 7 in one source and Day 14 in another) and a Weekend Amulet making users 4% more likely to return a week later; figures unverified against Duolingo originals. **[AGG]** — [Taplytics](https://taplytics.com/blog/how-duolingo-ran-an-experiment-on-their-streaks-feature); [JTBD UX write-up incl. backlash](https://jtbdux-protocol.pages.dev/adoption-and-behavior/behavior-design-patterns/how-duolingo-engineered-50-million-daily-users-and-the-backlash-that-came-with-it)
- Gamification of learning meta-analysis (Sailer & Homner, *Educational Psychology Review* 2020): cognitive outcomes g = 0.49 (k = 19, N = 1,686); motivation g = 0.36 (k = 16, N = 2,246); behavioural effects less stable and moderated by game fiction and social interaction; cognitive effect held in the most rigorous subset. **[PR]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)

**Notifications**
- Yancey & Settles (Duolingo), KDD 2020, "A Sleeping, Recovering Bandit Algorithm for Optimizing Recurring Notifications": a softmax bandit variant handling template eligibility ("sleeping arms," e.g., needing a 3+ day streak) and novelty decay ("recovering arms") with a recency penalty; success metric = lesson completed within 2 hours of the push; deployed in production; ~200 million notification examples released for replication. **[PR, industry]** — [Duolingo Research PDF](https://research.Duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf); [Duolingo blog explanation](https://blog.duolingo.com/hi-its-duo-the-ai-behind-the-meme/)
- Academic criticism (Brazilian HCI paper, via secondary) flags Duolingo's excessive notifications and emotionally charged visuals aimed at child users as manipulative. **[AGG]** — [JTBD UX write-up](https://jtbdux-protocol.pages.dev/adoption-and-behavior/behavior-design-patterns/how-duolingo-engineered-50-million-daily-users-and-the-backlash-that-came-with-it)

**Documented harms summary**
- Compulsive use (not duration) is the driver of cognitive correlates (see KQ1, Nguyen et al. 2025). — [PsyPost](https://www.psypost.org/large-meta-analysis-links-tiktok-and-instagram-reels-to-poorer-cognitive-and-mental-health/)
- Procrastination, stress, sleep disruption associated with habitual short-video use. — [SAGE](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21522715251404199)
- Reduced learning motivation and commitment among addicted students. — [MDPI](https://mdpi.com/2227-9032/11/7/985)

### Inferences
- Likes/comments operate as a Skinner-style reinforcement for *posting*, not for *learning*; in a workplace product they will train employees to produce content rather than to retain it unless the reward is tied to a learning action (e.g., answering a recall question).
- Autoplay and algorithmic recommendation are the two mechanics with the clearest evidence of eroding user agency; swipe gestures per se have little direct evidence either way.
- Streak effects at Duolingo are real but small in absolute terms (+0.38% DAU, +1.7% D7 retention per feature); the large aggregate comes from hundreds of stacked experiments, which a new product cannot replicate quickly.

### Gaps
- No study was found isolating the learning (as opposed to engagement) effect of autoplay, infinite scroll, or swipe in an instructional context.
- No peer-reviewed evaluation of streak mechanics on knowledge retention was found; Duolingo's figures are first-party and unaudited.
- No experimental evidence on social proof (likes/comments) in corporate learning feeds.

## KQ3. "Healthy hooks" and ethical persuasive design: what frameworks exist (Eyal Hooked/Indistractable, Center for Humane Technology, Duolingo, Octalysis) and what do they recommend for learning products?

### Takeaway
The frameworks converge on three tests: build only hooks the designer would use and that materially improve users' lives (Eyal), prefer intrinsic "white-hat" drives over scarcity/loss "black-hat" drives (Chou), and protect attention by aligning the product with user intention and adding friction where attention and intention diverge (CHT); Duolingo's practice adds a fourth rule — soften loss mechanics with "slack" (freezes) so streaks motivate without punishing.

### Cited Findings
- Nir Eyal's Manipulation Matrix asks two questions — "Will I use the product myself?" and "Will the product help users materially improve their lives?" — and is meant to answer "Should I attempt to hook users?" rather than "Can I?"; a product the designer would not use and that does not help users is "exploitation." **[PRACT]** — [Designli explainer](https://designli.co/blog/using-the-manipulation-matrix-for-ethical-behavioral-design); [Eyal in TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=581552); [Hooked chapter summary](https://supersummary.com/hooked/chapters-6-8-summary)
- Yu-kai Chou's Octalysis: eight core drives arranged on an octagon, split into white-hat (meaning, accomplishment, empowerment/creativity) and black-hat (scarcity, unpredictability, loss-avoidance) drives; Chou states "yes, gamification is a form of manipulation" and argues manipulation is ordinary in daily life; reviewers find the white/black-hat distinction the most useful part. **[PRACT]** — [Octalysis framework](https://medium.com/@yukaichou/the-octalysis-framework-for-gamification-behavioral-design-fe381150f0c1); [Chou on ethics](https://yukaichou.com/gamification-study/gamification-manipulation-ethics/)
- Center for Humane Technology: "Protect attention" principle = design that increases users' control over their own attention; recommends adding delays or friction to keep attention and intention aligned; reduce frequent context-switching and undifferentiated choices; its Design Guide addresses human "sensitivities" (emotional, attention, social reasoning). **[PRACT]** — [CHT Substack](https://humanetech.substack.com/p/how-to-thrive-with-tech); [CHT redesign post](https://humanetech.substack.com/p/redesigning-chatgpt-slack-and-xtwitter); [CHT humane product design page](https://center-for-humane-technology.vercel.app/humane-product-design)
- Lukoff et al. (CHI 2021) design implication: when users have a specific intention, offer agency-supporting controls (search, playlists) over autoplay/recommendations. **[PR]** — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11778)
- Duolingo's stated design rationale: streaks work via context-repetition habit formation and loss aversion, but rigid rules demotivate, so Streak Freeze "slack" (citing Penn/UCLA research) increases motivation; milestone celebrations rather than punishments. **[VENDOR]** — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/)
- Duolingo's notification bandit explicitly penalises recently-sent templates to avoid over-using any one message, "which goes against how standard bandit algorithms behave." **[VENDOR/PR]** — [Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/hi-its-duo-the-ai-behind-the-meme/)
- Sailer & Homner (2020): behavioural effects of gamification depend on game fiction and social interaction; combining competition with collaboration may help motivation. **[PR]** — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)

### Inferences
- A workplace learning feed passes Eyal's matrix only if the hook (e.g., a daily recall streak) improves job performance, not just app opens; the team should define "material improvement" as a measurable knowledge or behaviour outcome before shipping any hook.
- Octalysis suggests a pragmatic split: use black-hat drives (streak loss, limited-time challenges) only to *start* a habit in the first 7 days (Duolingo's fragile window) and shift to white-hat drives (mastery, meaning, autonomy) for sustained use.
- CHT's "intention alignment" maps directly onto Lukoff's finding: a learning feed should offer an explicit intent entry point (search, "my path," manager-assigned playlist) and treat the algorithmic "For You" feed as secondary.

### Gaps
- The Center for Humane Technology Design Guide text itself could not be retrieved; its exact attention guidelines are known only through secondary descriptions.
- No empirical comparison of "white-hat" vs "black-hat" mechanics on learning outcomes was found.
- Eyal's *Indistractable* content (the user-side antidote) did not surface directly; only the Hooked-era Manipulation Matrix was sourced.

## KQ4. Evidence on in-video retrieval (interpolated questions), captions, duration sweet spot, vertical vs horizontal format, and speed controls

### Takeaway
Interpolated quizzes reliably improve retention of tested material (large lab effects; smaller, inconsistent effects on mind-wandering in replications); viewer engagement drops sharply beyond ~6 minutes in MOOC data and halves within 1-minute marketing clips; playback up to 2× does not hurt comprehension of straightforward lecture content; captions help second-language and hard-of-hearing viewers with weaker evidence for neurotypical native speakers; and no controlled study establishes that vertical beats horizontal for recall.

### Cited Findings

**Interpolated questions / in-video retrieval**
- Szpunar, Khan & Schacter (2013), *PNAS*: during a 21-minute recorded statistics lecture, mind-wandering was reported on ~40% of probes in non-tested/restudy groups versus ~20% in the interpolated-test group; tested students took more notes and retained more. **[CLASSIC]** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699/)
- Jing, Szpunar & Schacter (2016), 40-minute lecture: mind-wandering rates did not differ between tested and restudy groups, but mind-wandering was more detrimental to final performance in the groups that did it more. **[CLASSIC]** — [Harvard PDF](https://sites.harvard.edu/schacter-memory/files/2022/08/jing2016-1-1.pdf)
- Welhaf et al. (WashU), 195 undergraduates: interpolated testing "might have limited utility to support students' attention," with varying effect sizes warranting further work. **[PR]** — [WUSTL profile](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/interpolated-testing-and-content-pretesting-as-interventions-to-r/)
- Pan et al. (2020): both interpolated and conventional pretesting improved learning and reduced mind-wandering versus controls. **[PR]** — [PubMed](https://www.pubmed.gov/35348931/)
- Interpolating online lectures with quiz questions enhanced learning for both community-college and university students; benefit moderated by distraction level; authors note all mind-wandering studies were lab-based. **[PR]** — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11965562)
- Classroom quizzing meta-analysis (Yang et al. 2021): g ≈ 0.50 (see KQ1). — [NSF PDF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10518320)

**Duration sweet spot**
- Guo, Kim & Rubin (2014): 6.9 million edX video-watching sessions across four courses; median engagement time ~6 minutes regardless of video length; students watched on average only ~3 minutes of videos 12+ minutes long; recommendation: chunk under 6 minutes. **[CLASSIC, via secondary]** — [SUNY OSCQR summary](https://oscqr.suny.edu/?p=3213); [Education Designs summary](https://educationdesignsinc.com/lecture-capture/)
- A 2022 systematic review of 113 studies supports segmentation and reducing unnecessary length but no specific threshold; no universal optimum established. **[PR, via secondary]** — [SUNY OSCQR](https://oscqr.suny.edu/?p=3213)
- Wistia (vendor, >80 million videos): ~50% of viewers watch the entirety of sub-1-minute videos; 2026 report gives 52% *engagement rate* (share of runtime watched) for under-1-minute videos; 2024 report: 43% engagement for 3–5-minute videos overall, 74% for instructional 3–5-minute videos. **[VENDOR]** — [Wistia optimal length](https://wistia.com/blog/optimal-video-length); [Wistia 2024 State of Video](https://wistia.com/blog/insights-state-of-video-report); [Wistia 2012 length analysis](https://wistia.com/blog/does-length-matter-it-does-for-video-2k12-edition)
- Easygenerator benchmark: e-learning courses of 5–15 minutes reached 81.5% completion vs 70.6% for >15 minutes. **[VENDOR]** — [Easygenerator](https://www.easygenerator.com/en/blog/e-learning/e-learning-benchmark-report/)
- Donald Clark (2021): micro-videos range from ~15 s (TikTok) to ~6 minutes; cites drop-off around 6 minutes; argues brevity limits cognitive overload. **[PRACT]** — [Plan B blog](https://donaldclarkplanb.blogspot.com/2021/04/micro-videos-what-are-they-how-to-make.html)
- Clark Quinn rejects attention-limit-based caps ("as long as it needs to be, and no longer") and faults articles citing a "3–10 minutes of attention span" figure without evidence. **[PRACT]** — [Learnlets 2018](https://blog.learnlets.com/2018/03/diagramming-microlearning/); [Learnlets 2022](https://blog.learnlets.com/2022/02/good-and-bad-advice-all-in-one/)
- TalentLMS notes TikTok-length (60 s) may be too short for training; a 3-minute video can still support engagement and retention. **[VENDOR]** — [TalentLMS](https://www.talentlms.com/blog/tiktok-for-learning/)

**Speed controls**
- Murphy et al. (2022), *Applied Cognitive Psychology*, UCLA: undergraduates watched lectures at 1×, 1.5×, 2×, 2.5×; comprehension immediately and one week later was not significantly reduced up to 2×; performance dropped at 2.5×; re-watching at 2× just before the test improved scores vs one 1× viewing a week earlier (differences in some comparisons not significant); students believed slower was better; authors caution for complex material. Reported raw scores differ across outlets. **[PR]** — [ScienceDaily](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2022/01/220111153637.htm); [UMPI CTL summary](https://www.umpi.edu/ctl/?p=5084)

**Captions**
- Gernsbacher (2015) review: >100 empirical studies document that captioning improves comprehension of, attention to, and memory for video; benefits strongest for non-native-language viewers, children/adults learning to read, and D/deaf or hard-of-hearing viewers; eye-tracking shows captions are read easily and integrated smoothly with audio. **[CLASSIC review]** — [ADA Accessibility reprint](https://www.adaccessibility.org/adaccessibility/2015/10/01/video-captions-benefit-everyone)
- Critique (Watson, Learning and the Brain): the cited studies focus on foreign-language captions, non-neurotypical learners, college students, or self-report; found no research with objective measures for neurotypical K-12 learners reading same-language captions; cautions that captions add load on already-complex videos. **[PRACT]** — [Learning and the Brain](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/video-captions-benefit-everyone-an-investigation/)

**Vertical vs horizontal**
- University of Agder 2024 master's thesis (N = 104): vertical mobile educational video produced a stronger sense of personal connection and was preferred by most of its viewers; recommended for short, introductory, illustration-only content; recall outcomes not reported in the abstract. **[PP]** — [UiA repository](https://uia.brage.unit.no/uia-xmlui/handle/11250/3139018)
- ESPOL (Ecuador) 2024: students using YouTube Shorts-style micro-videos preferred *horizontal* format with shorter duration. **[PP]** — [ESPOL DSpace](https://dspace.espol.edu.ec/handle/123456789/62862)

### Inferences
- The "tested material" caveat matters: interpolated questions mainly lift retention of what was asked, so a reels product should quiz the one key point of each clip, not peripheral facts.
- Combining Wistia (50% finish sub-1-minute clips) with Guo (6-minute median engagement) suggests a sweet spot of roughly 45–180 s per clip for content that must be finished, with 3–6 minutes tolerable for explicitly instructional content viewers chose.
- A 2× speed control is low-risk for procedural/declarative content and signals respect for user time; it should be disabled or capped at 1.5× for complex conceptual clips.
- Captions should be on by default (workplace viewing is often muted; many frontline workforces are multilingual), accepting that the evidence for native-speaker benefit is weaker than the headline claim.

### Gaps
- No controlled study compares recall from vertical vs horizontal educational video; preference evidence conflicts.
- No study tests interpolated questions inside sub-2-minute swipe-feed clips (all research uses 20–40-minute lectures).
- Guo et al. (2014) and the 2022 113-study review were accessed only via secondary summaries.

## KQ6. What do L&D practitioners and critics (Bersin, Thalheimer, Shank, Quinn, Donald Clark) and ATD say about "TikTok-style" corporate learning?

### Takeaway
Practitioners broadly accept short, in-the-flow formats but reject the attention-span rationale: Quinn insists "microlearning" conflates spaced learning with performance support and that chunking without spacing is not learning; Thalheimer's spacing and retrieval work is the scientific backbone vendors invoke; Clark endorses sub-6-minute micro-videos; Bersin frames micro as "solving problems" and macro as "building skills"; ATD says TikTok itself is unviable for corporate use but its design lessons are worth borrowing.

### Cited Findings
- Clark Quinn (Jan 2026): "the problem is that there are regularly two things meant: either spaced learning or performance support. Both are good things, but lumping them under one label constitutes a problem." **[PRACT]** — [Learnlets](https://blog.learnlets.com/2026/01/ideas-we-could-do-without/)
- Quinn (2022): 3–5-minute videos are not microlearning on his definition; critiques articles that cite "3–10 minutes of attention span" with no data. **[PRACT]** — [Learnlets](https://blog.learnlets.com/2022/02/good-and-bad-advice-all-in-one/)
- Quinn (2018): chunks should be "as long as [they need] to be, and no longer" (siding with JD Dillon); simply chunking a course "isn't taking the decay of memory over time and the gradual expansion of capability" into account. **[PRACT]** — [Learnlets](https://blog.learnlets.com/2018/03/diagramming-microlearning/)
- Will Thalheimer: 2006 spacing research report; "Decisive Dozen" places spacing and retrieval practice among the key learning factors; claims implementing retrieval practice, spacing, context alignment and feedback could roughly double learning effectiveness (promotional claim); "subscription learning" model relies on push technology and the spacing effect. **[PRACT]** — [Work-Learning Research](https://www.worklearning.com/2017/01/07/wills-history-of-spacing-out-on-learning/); [2006 report](https://Gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/2006-thalheimer.pdf); [Downes live-blog of 2014 talk](https://www.downes.ca/post/61927/rd)
- Donald Clark (Plan B, 2021): micro-videos (15 s to ~6 min) limit cognitive overload and aid retention; education has adopted YouTube, Facebook and TikTok; produces 90-second examples. **[PRACT]** — [Plan B blog](https://donaldclarkplanb.blogspot.com/2021/04/micro-videos-what-are-they-how-to-make.html)
- Josh Bersin: "microlearning solves problems; macrolearning develops new skills"; coined "learning in the flow of work" (2018); flow-of-work also means mentors, after-action reviews and reflective questioning, not only short content. **[PRACT]** — [CLO Media](https://www.chieflearningofficer.com/2018/04/02/learning-flow-work); [CLO "Harnessing Hyperconnectivity"](https://www.clomedia.com/2018/05/21/harnessing-hyperconnectivity/)
- Patti Shank (2018), "Microlearning, macrolearning: what does the research tell us?" (eLearning Industry) — cited but text not retrieved. **[PRACT]** — [reference listing](https://sites.google.com/view/trendsmicrolearning/references)
- ATD: "TikTok isn't currently a viable platform for corporate learning due to privacy and security concerns," but its design lessons are worth borrowing. **[PRACT, partner content]** — [ATD](https://www.td.org/content/professional-partner-content/how-tiktok-is-influencing-the-future-of-elearning)
- "The TikTok Trap: Why Workplace Learning Isn't Just a Game of Short Attention Spans" (IDTips Substack) argues against "TikTok is the future of learning." **[PRACT]** — [IDTips](https://idtips.substack.com/p/the-tiktok-trap)
- Learning Guild / vendor commentary ("Tiktokification of Learning," TalentLMS, EduMe) promotes borrowing TikTok's hook-first, creator-led, vertical format. **[VENDOR/PRACT]** — [Learning Guild](https://www.learningguild.com/?p=23983); [TalentLMS](https://www.talentlms.com/blog/tiktok-inspired-training/); [EduMe](https://www.edume.com/blog/tiktok-style-employee-training)
- Jane Bozarth (Learning Guild, 2024) research report: microlearning's anticipated benefits appear realised across the (small) research base. **[PRACT research report]** — [Learning Guild PDF](https://cedma-europe.org/newsletter%20articles/eLearning%20Guild/Microlearning%20-%20Current%20State%20and%20Future%20Implications%20(Apr%2024).pdf)

### Inferences
- The practitioner consensus supports a reels product *if* it is explicitly a spaced-retrieval system (Quinn/Thalheimer) or a performance-support lookup tool (Bersin's "solve problems"), and opposes it if marketed as a replacement for skill-building macrolearning.
- Critics' main objection is the attention-span rationale, not short video per se; the product narrative should lead with spacing/retrieval science and time-scarcity (24 min/week), not "Gen Z attention spans."

### Gaps
- No direct statement from Josh Bersin, Will Thalheimer or Patti Shank specifically about TikTok-style feeds was found; their positions above are inferred from their microlearning/spacing writing.
- Patti Shank's 2018 evidence review text was not retrieved.

## KQ7. Design implications for a workplace learning reels product

### Takeaway
Keep the clip format and the frictionless capture of attention, but replace every compulsion mechanic with a learning mechanic: quiz-gated progress instead of autoplay, intention-first navigation instead of a pure "For You" feed, spaced re-surfacing instead of infinite novelty, soft streaks with freezes, bandit-timed but capped notifications, and session caps aligned to the 24-minutes-a-week reality.

### Cited Findings (design-implications table)

| Mechanic | Evidence for (engagement and/or learning) | Risk | Recommended implementation for a workplace learning reels product |
|---|---|---|---|
| Short clip length (15–90 s; up to ~3–6 min for chosen instructional content) | Median engagement ~6 min in 6.9M MOOC sessions ([Guo via OSCQR](https://oscqr.suny.edu/?p=3213)); ~50% of viewers finish sub-1-minute videos, 74% engagement for 3–5 min instructional ([Wistia](https://wistia.com/blog/optimal-video-length), [Wistia 2024](https://wistia.com/blog/insights-state-of-video-report)); 42% of employees prefer <3-minute learning videos ([inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/employees-are-turning-to-tiktok-for-career-development-training)) | "Nano" clips may be too short for anything beyond a single fact ([An-Najah](https://staff.najah.edu/en/publications/12503/)); chunking without spacing is not learning ([Quinn](https://blog.learnlets.com/2018/03/diagramming-microlearning/)) | One learning objective per clip; default 45–90 s for feed clips; allow 2–5 min "deep" clips reached by intent (search/path), not autoplay; never cap length by an attention-span argument |
| Interpolated retrieval question after each clip | Mind-wandering ~20% vs ~40% with interpolated tests ([Szpunar 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3631699/)); classroom quizzing g ≈ 0.50 over 222 studies ([Yang 2021 via NSF](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10518320)); benefits community-college and university online learners ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11965562)) | Replications show smaller/inconsistent attention effects ([Welhaf](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/interpolated-testing-and-content-pretesting-as-interventions-to-r/)); benefit mostly for *tested* material | Make the next swipe pass through a 1-item recall question on the clip's key point with immediate feedback; use the answer as the "reward" event, not a like |
| Spaced re-surfacing (algorithmic repetition) | Spacing effect: 839 assessments, 317 experiments ([Cepeda 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/)); Thalheimer subscription-learning model ([Work-Learning](https://www.worklearning.com/2017/01/07/wills-history-of-spacing-out-on-learning/)) | Feed novelty bias pushes toward endless new content rather than repetition | Personalise the feed on *forgetting risk*, not on watch-time; re-insert previously failed items at expanding intervals (1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 1 month) |
| Autoplay | Drives time-on-app on consumer platforms ([scoping review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2212657026000760)) | Primarily undermines user sense of agency ([Lukoff CHI 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11778)); friction on autoplay reduces compulsive use ([review summary](https://note.com/jpss4691/n/n3959f2e4c977?hl=en)); CHT: add friction where attention and intention diverge ([CHT](https://humanetech.substack.com/p/how-to-thrive-with-tech)) | Autoplay *within* a chosen playlist/path only; between clips in the open feed require a swipe or quiz answer; no autoplay on first open |

| Swipe gesture | Low-friction navigation; preference and "personal connection" for vertical in one thesis ([UiA](https://uia.brage.unit.no/uia-xmlui/handle/11250/3139018)) | Swipe/recommendation increased task switching in children ([review summary](https://note.com/jpss4691/n/n3959f2e4c977?hl=en)); conflicting format preference ([ESPOL](https://dspace.espol.edu.ec/handle/123456789/62862)) | Keep vertical swipe for discovery; add swipe-up for "show me the source doc / job aid" so the gesture serves performance support; A/B test vertical vs horizontal recall because no study settles it |
| Algorithmic personalisation ("For You") | Drives consumer engagement; Duolingo's bandit personalises notification *timing/content* effectively ([KDD 2020](https://research.Duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)) | Recommendations reduce agency ([Lukoff](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.11778)); users with intent prefer search/playlists | Default landing = role-based path + manager assignments + spaced reviews; "For You" discovery as a secondary tab; personalise on knowledge gaps and job role, never on watch-time maximisation |
| Social proof (likes, comments, creator model) | Likes act as reinforcement for posting; social rewards causally shape behaviour ([Lindström 2021](https://research.vu.nl/en/publications/a-computational-reward-learning-account-of-social-media-engagemen/)); gamification behavioural effects depend on social interaction ([Sailer & Homner](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w)) | Reinforces content *production* and comparison, not retention; black-hat drive if tied to status | Replace likes with "this helped me on the job" and "I tried it" signals; show comments as Q&A threads with SME answers; recognise creators on helpfulness, not views |

| Notifications | Bandit-optimised pushes with recency penalty, success = lesson within 2 h ([KDD 2020](https://research.Duolingo.com/papers/yancey.kdd20.pdf)); "commit to my goal" copy raised retention ([Lenny's](https://lennysnewsletter.com/p/behind-the-product-duolingo-streaks)) | Excessive/emotional notifications criticised as manipulative ([JTBD UX](https://jtbdux-protocol.pages.dev/adoption-and-behavior/behavior-design-patterns/how-duolingo-engineered-50-million-daily-users-and-the-backlash-that-came-with-it)); workplace context-switch cost (47-s screen attention, [UC](https://universityofcalifornia.edu/news/how-sharpen-your-attention-and-meet-your-goals-2024)) | Max 1 push per working day, within user-chosen or shift-aware windows; content = a spaced-recall item due today, not a guilt message; deliver via existing work channels (Teams/Slack) where possible |
| Captions on by default | >100 studies show captions aid comprehension, attention, memory, strongest for non-native speakers ([Gernsbacher 2015](https://www.adaccessibility.org/adaccessibility/2015/10/01/video-captions-benefit-everyone)) | Weaker evidence for neurotypical native speakers; extra load on complex visuals ([Watson](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/video-captions-benefit-everyone-an-investigation/)) | Burned-in or toggleable captions on by default (muted workplace viewing, multilingual frontline); keep on-screen text minimal when the visual is dense |

| Ethical gate (Eyal matrix / Octalysis / CHT) | Eyal: "Would I use it? Does it materially improve users' lives?" ### Inferences
- The product's north-star metric should be delayed recall accuracy (e.g., 7-day and 30-day spaced-quiz performance) and manager-reported application, with time-on-app treated as a *cost* metric to minimise, inverting the consumer-feed objective function.
- Benchmarks to set: ≥50% completion for sub-60 s clips, ≥75% for intent-chosen 2–5 min clips, ≥70% weekly active among assigned users (mandatory-use context), 3–8-minute median session, ≤1 push/day, and a spaced-recall accuracy target rather than a DAU/MAU target.
- The strongest evidence-backed differentiators versus a plain LMS are (1) interpolated retrieval and (2) spaced re-surfacing; the strongest differentiators versus TikTok are (1) finite sessions and (2) intention-first navigation.

### Gaps
- No field study exists of a workplace vertical-video feed with these mechanics; every recommendation above is an inference from adjacent evidence and should be validated with an internal A/B programme (Duolingo's precedent: 600+ experiments on streaks alone).
- Effect sizes for most mechanics on *learning* (not engagement) are unknown; teams should expect modest per-feature lifts (Duolingo's +0.4% to +1.7%) rather than step changes.
