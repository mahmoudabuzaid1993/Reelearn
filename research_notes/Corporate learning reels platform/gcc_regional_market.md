# Saudi Arabia and GCC market for a B2B workplace-learning "reels" product

Scope note: Research conducted 29 Oct 2026. Where a figure is older than 2025 it is marked "(older data: YEAR)". Saudi Arabia (KSA) is treated separately from the other GCC states wherever sources allow. Many of the best regional sources are consultancy/vendor pieces rather than primary texts; this is flagged inline. Two HRDF PDF program guides (2025 and 2026 editions) failed to fetch, so cost-share percentages for training products are a gap.

## Key Question 1: Demand drivers (Vision 2030 / HCDP, HRDF subsidies, Saudization, national upskilling programs, mandated training, market sizing)

### Takeaway
The GCC corporate-training market is sized at roughly USD 12.9B in 2025 (KSA about 45% of it, growing ~10%/yr), with Vision 2030 human-capital agendas, nationalisation quotas (Nitaqat, Emiratisation, the new Qatarization law) and heavy state co-funding (HRDF spent SAR 3.77B in H1 2025 alone; Bahrain's Tamkeen covers up to 100% of training cost for nationals) acting as demand engines that have no equivalent in a global playbook. Precise HRDF cost-share percentages for digital training products could not be confirmed from primary documents.

### Cited Findings

**Market sizing (multiple research houses, scopes differ)**
- GCC corporate training market valued at USD 12.88B in 2025, USD 13.94B in 2026, projected USD 22.21B by 2031 (CAGR 9.76% 2026–2031); Saudi Arabia held 45.12% of the GCC market in 2025; UAE is the fastest-growing at 11.12% CAGR — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/gcc-corporate-training-market)
- Same source, 2025 segment splits: technical/digital skills 40.12% of training type; online self-paced 55.23% of delivery; large enterprises 65.19% (SMEs growing fastest at 13.11% CAGR); BFSI 35.23% of end-user demand; VILT fastest-growing delivery at 12.64% CAGR — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/gcc-corporate-training-market)
- Same source lists drivers with estimated CAGR impact: Vision 2030/human-capital agendas (+3.2 pts), enterprise digital/AI upskilling (+2.8), shift to online/blended (+1.8), leadership/soft skills (+1.2), localisation-quota enforcement (+0.9), demand for bilingual Arabic-English enablement (+0.4). Restraints: ROI scrutiny (−1.4), low learner engagement in digital-first programs (−0.8), shortage of Arabic-English facilitators and localised content (−0.6), data-residency/explainable-AI compliance (−0.3) — [Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/gcc-corporate-training-market)
- KSA-only corporate training market: ~USD 3.8B in 2025, CAGR 6.75% to USD 6.0B by 2032 — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/industry-reports/saudi-arabia-corporate-education-and-training-market); USD 3.59B in 2024, CAGR 7.10% 2025–2033 — [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/saudi-arabia-corporate-training-market); a narrower Ken Research "executive education and corporate training" report (Oct 2025) gives USD 1.2B — [Ken Research](https://www.kenresearch.com/saudi-arabia-executive-education-and-corporate-training-market). These are paid-report teaser figures with differing scope; treat as order-of-magnitude only.

### Inferences
- The structural buyer motive in KSA is quota compliance plus subsidy capture: employers must hire and retain more Saudis (Nitaqat phase 2, 269 raised quotas, 100% rules for admin roles) and HRDF co-funds the "qualification" cost. A reels product that produces auditable training records for Saudi employees is more fundable than one positioned purely on engagement.
- HRDF's own Doroob is both a channel (employers already have portals and admin roles there) and a free competitor for generic content; differentiation must be company-specific, job-specific or format-led.
- Mordor's stated restraints map directly to the product's pitch: low engagement in digital-first programs (−0.8 pts) and shortage of Arabic content (−0.6 pts) are the problems a short-form, Arabic-first feed claims to solve; ROI scrutiny (−1.4 pts) means analytics/compliance evidence must be first-class.
- Giga-projects run their own academies (TVTC-accredited diplomas, degrees) for Saudi hiring pipelines; the realistic entry for a reels product there is onboarding/safety/service refreshers for the operating workforce, not the academy curriculum.

## Key Question 2: Buyers and procurement (who buys, Etimad, local content/RHQ, PDPL and cloud rules, sales cycles, SIs/resellers)

### Takeaway
Government and semi-government buyers purchase via Etimad and require commercial registration, a LCGPA Local Content Certificate and (until a 2026 exemption mechanism) a Saudi regional HQ; private buyers are governed by the PDPL, whose cross-border transfer rules are now under enforcement and push vendors to in-Kingdom hosting (STC Cloud, Mobily, Google Cloud KSA region). Hard evidence on typical sales-cycle length and the share of deals that flow through SIs/resellers was not found.

### Cited Findings
- KSA government procurement: all bidders need Saudi commercial registration, Etimad registration and sector classifications; a Local Content Certificate from LCGPA (applied for via the LCGPA portal linked to Etimad, valid 19 months) is required before bidding on qualifying tenders; scoring pillars include share of Saudi nationals employed; contractors failing local-content thresholds are disqualified — [OpenOpps](https://openopps.com/blog/how-to-win-government-contracts-and-tenders-in-saudi-arabia-2026); [Setup in Saudi (consultancy)](https://www.setupinsaudi.com/news/announcements/what-foreign-investors-should-know-about-saudi-local-content-rules); [Astrolabs](https://insight.astrolabs.com/turning-local-content-into-a-strategic-advantage-in-saudi-arabia)
- 2026 change: KSA introduced an exemption mechanism allowing government entities to contract with foreign companies lacking a regional HQ, requested via a dedicated Etimad e-service — [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/new-rule-saudi-arabia-reverses-restriction-allows-foreign-companies-in-government-projects-1.500451195); [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/node/5242696)
- A "Mandatory List" gives local products priority in government procurement — [Asharq Al-Awsat](https://english.aawsat.com/business/5162901-saudi-arabia%E2%80%99s-mandatory-list-boosts-local-companies-government-procurement)
- US Commercial Service: some government-controlled enterprises add their own requirements for foreign firms beyond LCGPA rules — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/country-commercial-guides/saudi-arabia-trade-barriers)
- PDPL cross-border rules are "now under enforcement"; US trade agency summary says companies must store sensitive and personally identifiable data in KSA unless exemptions apply, and global cloud models may need restructuring — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/saudi-arabia-ict-cross-border-data-transfer-rules-now-under-enforcement)
- Baker McKenzie: beyond the PDPL and Data Transfer Regulations there are sectoral rules focused on data localisation — [Baker McKenzie](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/emea/saudi-arabia/topics/data-localization-and-regulation-of-non-personal-data)
- SDAIA issued a Risk Assessment Guideline for transfers outside KSA in Feb 2025; Data Transfer Regulations set minimum elements (purpose, legal basis); as of mid-2025 SDAIA's adequacy list had not been published; SCCs and BCRs are available safeguards (BCRs only for affiliated parties) — [Clyde & Co](https://www.clydeco.com/en/insights/2025/03/update-on-saudi-arabia-risk-assessment-guidelines); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/saudiarabia/data-protection/1641982/saudi-arabias-personal-data-protection-law-a-comprehensive-overview-of-the-legal-and-regulatory-framework); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/insights/articles/international-personal-data-transfers-under-saudi-arabias-data-protection-law)
- Local hosting options cited: Google Cloud KSA region; STC Cloud and Mobily sovereign cloud — [Upgrowth (vendor blog)](https://upgrowth.in/saudi-saas-compliance-checklist/)
- CST Cloud Computing Services Provisioning Regulations in force since 10 Oct 2023 (v4 current); CSPs and subscribers are prohibited from transferring Saudi government-agency data outside the Kingdom in any form; NDMO/SDAIA data classification directly affects which CSP category can be used; CST launched a Cloud Computing Market Maturity Index on 3 Sep 2026 — [CST](https://cst.gov.sa/en/RulesandSystems/RegulatoryDocuments/Pages/CCRF.aspx); [Chambers Cloud Computing 2026](https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/cloud-computing-2026/saudi-arabia/trends-and-developments); [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/25026-implemented-cloud-computing-services-provisioning-regulations-including-data-localisation-requirement); NCA cloud cybersecurity controls with localisation requirements adopted Feb 2021 (older data) — [Digital Policy Alert](https://digitalpolicyalert.org/change/5275-data-localisation-requirements-in-cloud-cybersecurity-controls)
- Axonify's Middle East operations are run by Biz Group (Dubai) and its platform is hosted in KSA on Google Cloud for data-sovereignty compliance (software-directory claim, unverified with vendor) — [ZoftwareHub](https://zoftwarehub.com/en-sa/products/axonify/overview)
- UAE: Federal Decree-Law 45/2021 (PDPL) applies to processing and transfers; several vendor sources say sector rules (health, financial, government; CBUAE and TDRA guidance) drive in-country residency more than the PDPL itself; AWS/Azure/Google have UAE and Qatar regions — [InCountry (vendor)](https://incountry.com/blog/data-sovereignty-in-the-uae/); [MomentumX (vendor)](https://momentumx.cloud/blog/uae-pdpl-implementation-guide-cloud-workloads-2026/)
- Qatar: PDPPL restricts cross-border transfers absent adequate protection; health-sector rules require local storage; QCB issued cloud rules for banks/fintechs in 2024 — [Kiteworks (vendor)](https://www.kiteworks.com/regulatory-compliance/qatar-healthcare-data-residency-compliance/)
- Pricing-page evidence of VAT treatment: a regional LMS quotes prices "excluding 5% UAE VAT and 15% Saudi VAT" — [Mindcat Learn pricing via SaaSworthy](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/mindcat-learn/pricing)

### Inferences
- Segment the buyer map: (a) government/semi-government and PIF-portfolio entities (Etimad, LCGPA certificate, government-data localisation absolute); (b) large family groups and listed corporates (PDPL-driven; will ask for in-Kingdom hosting and Arabic contracts); (c) SMEs (94% of HRDF-supported establishments; buy via HRIS ecosystems and resellers). A reels vendor without KSA hosting will be screened out of (a) and most of (b).
- The 2026 RHQ-exemption mechanism lowers but does not remove the bar; the practical path for a foreign vendor remains a Saudi entity or a licensed local reseller that holds the Etimad/LCGPA credentials.
- Because employee video-viewing logs and quiz results are personal data of Saudi data subjects, the default architecture should be KSA-resident tenants (Google Cloud KSA, STC/Mobily sovereign cloud, or Oracle/AWS KSA) with SCCs for any support access from abroad.

### Gaps
- No quantitative evidence on typical L&D SaaS sales cycle length in KSA/GCC or on the share of enterprise deals routed through SIs/resellers (no source found).
- Whether the SDAIA adequacy list has been published since mid-2025 (not verified).
- Specific NCA Essential Cybersecurity Controls / Cloud Cybersecurity Controls requirements that a private-sector SaaS vendor must certify against (primary text not retrieved).
- KSA VAT registration obligations for a non-resident SaaS provider selling B2B (not researched; only the 15% rate is evidenced).

## Key Question 3: Local and regional competitors

### Takeaway
No Arabic-first "reels for learning" or vertical-video microlearning startup for employers was found in English or Arabic searches; the regional field consists of Almentor (video course library, USD 14.5M+ raised, expanding into KSA), the free government platform Doroob, global catalogues (Coursera with ~4,000 Arabic courses and a million Saudi learners), frontline microlearning imports (Axonify via Biz Group, Disprz with a Saudi push), and a long tail of classroom training houses.

### Inferences
- White space: there is no evidenced Arabic-native, employer-curated short-form video feed product in the GCC; the nearest substitutes are (i) Almentor-style long-form Arabic video catalogues, (ii) imported frontline microlearning (Axonify/Disprz/SC Training) sold through Dubai partners, and (iii) free Doroob. The product's defensible position is format plus Arabic/Gulf-dialect content plus local hosting, not catalogue breadth.
- Biz Group's role for Axonify and e& capital's stake in Almentor show that credible regional distribution tends to come through a Dubai-based partner or a telco investor.

### Gaps
- Funding, customer and pricing data for Edraak, Rwaq, Nafham, Abwaab, Noon Academy, Masarat, LinkedIn Learning Arabic, Udemy Business in KSA, Sana: not researched within the call budget (most are K-12/consumer and likely low relevance, but unconfirmed).
- Named Gulf customers of Axonify, Disprz, Almentor corporate: none found in public sources.
- No evidence found on whether Almentor's KSA expansion (announced 2023) materialised or on its current corporate customer count.

## Key Question 4: Workforce and language realities (devices, social video, WhatsApp, Arabic vs English, expatriate languages, cultural norms, Ramadan/prayer timing)

### Takeaway
KSA is a near-universal smartphone/social-video market (99% internet penetration, 140% mobile connections, YouTube reach 79% and Snapchat 72% of the population, TikTok ad reach above 100% of adults, WhatsApp used by ~94% of men and ~90% of women), but 77% of the employed population is expatriate, so an Arabic-first product must also plan for English and South/Southeast Asian languages; Ramadan hours are legally capped at six hours/day for fasting employees.

### Cited Findings

## Key Question 7: Regulatory and content constraints (GCAM/media licensing, CST app rules, moderation, data localisation, ZATCA e-invoicing)

### Takeaway
KSA's audiovisual media regime (GCAM, now under a Law of Audiovisual Media and a draft broader Media Law) licenses media activity including online platforms and sets local content standards; whether a closed, employer-only training video platform needs a GCAM licence is unresolved in the sources. ZATCA Phase 2 e-invoicing integration now reaches resident taxpayers with turnover above SAR 187,500 (wave 25, from 1 Feb 2027), so a Saudi-registered SaaS entity will need Fatoora-integrated invoicing almost from day one.

### Cited Findings
- Law of Audiovisual Media (published by MISA, Jul 2025 upload) names the General Commission for Audiovisual Media as regulator — [MISA PDF](https://misa.gov.sa/app/uploads/2025/07/Law-of-Audiovisual-Media.pdf); the regulations contemplate licensing of foreign streaming platforms and require audiovisual activities to comply with local content standards — [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/the-sun-always-shines-on-tv-media-licensing-and-content-regulation-in-saudi-arabia)
- Draft Media Law (consultation 2023): all media companies, including digital platforms, would need a licence and fee; implementing regulation to follow within 90 days of Council of Ministers approval, setting licensing process, fees and content restrictions (older data: 2023 draft; finalisation not confirmed) — [ASG analysis](https://albrightstonebridge.com/node/6256); [SMEX](https://smex.org/saudi-authorities-monopolize-media-and-advertising-discourse-by-law/)
- GCAM "Mawthooq" permit: from Oct 2022 every Saudi and non-Saudi creator earning advertising revenue on social media must hold a permit — [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2140406/media); [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.pk/node/2176661)
- 2025 GCAM content guidelines described by secondary sources as tightening online expression and aligning content with social values — [Mimeta (advocacy)](https://www.mimeta.org/mimeta-news-on-censorship-in-art/2025/10/21/saudi-arabias-2025-media-rules-tighten-online-expression); [Soul of Saudi (secondary)](https://soulofsaudi.com/?p=16834)
- ICLG KSA Telecoms, Media & Internet 2025 chapter exists as a reference for the licensing map — [BSA Law](https://bsalaw.com/insight/ksa-telecoms-media-and-internet-2025-iclg-18th-edition/)
- Data localisation: see KQ2 (PDPL transfer rules under enforcement; CST cloud regulations; government data cannot leave KSA) — [trade.gov](https://www.trade.gov/market-intelligence/saudi-arabia-ict-cross-border-data-transfer-rules-now-under-enforcement); [CST](https://cst.gov.sa/en/RulesandSystems/RegulatoryDocuments/Pages/CCRF.aspx)
- ZATCA Phase 2 (Fatoora integration) waves: wave 23 threshold SAR 750,000 (go-live 1 Jan–31 Mar 2026); wave 24 SAR 375,000 (1 Apr–30 Jun 2026); wave 25 SAR 187,500 in any of 2022–2025, compliance from 1 Feb 2027 (first wave to count 2025 revenue, so entities founded in 2025 can be in scope); ZATCA notifies at least six months ahead — [EY wave 25](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1705-saudi-arabia-announces-25th-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration); [EY wave 24](https://taxnews.ey.com/news/2025-2023-saudi-arabia-announces-24th-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration); [EY wave 23](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/saudi-arabia-announces-23rd-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration); [ZATCA wave 20](https://zatca.gov.sa/en/MediaCenter/News/Pages/e-invoicing-wave-20-2025.aspx)
- VAT: 15% in KSA, 5% in UAE (as reflected in regional SaaS pricing) — [SaaSworthy/Mindcat](https://www.saasworthy.com/product/mindcat-learn/pricing)

### Inferences
- A B2B platform distributing employer-selected training videos to a closed workforce is not obviously "media activity" in the broadcast sense, but the trend (draft Media Law covering digital platforms, 2025 content guidelines) means legal review of GCAM scope is a must-have before launch, and content must be pre-screened against local content standards regardless of licensing status.
- User-generated reels from employees (if the product allows it) raise the regulatory bar materially (moderation, Mawthooq-type questions if monetised); employer-curated content with no public distribution is the lower-risk design.
- Plan for ZATCA Phase 2 compliance (QR-coded, Fatoora-integrated tax invoices in Arabic) in the billing stack from the first Saudi invoice.

### Gaps
- Whether a closed B2B learning-video platform requires a GCAM licence (no source addresses this).
- Current status and text of the broader Media Law implementing regulations (not retrieved).
- CST app-level rules for enterprise apps (none found beyond the cloud regulations).
- Non-resident VAT registration and e-invoicing obligations for a foreign SaaS vendor billing Saudi businesses (not researched).

## Key Question 8: What differs from the global playbook (synthesis list)

### Takeaway
Compared with a US/EU frontline-learning launch, the GCC version is subsidy- and quota-driven, locally hosted, Arabic-first with multilingual expatriate layers, sold through Etimad/LCGPA and HRIS-telco partners, priced ex-VAT in SAR/AED, and subject to media-content standards and Ramadan/prayer rhythms.

### Cited Findings
- Subsidy economics: HRDF spent SAR 3.77B in H1 2025 across 136,000+ establishments (94% SMEs) — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1837800); Tamkeen covers up to 100% of Bahraini training cost — [Trade Arabia](https://tradearabia.com/News/297919/Arla-Foods-supports-career-growth-of-160-Bahrainis-through-Tamkeen-programs)

- Procurement gates (Etimad, LCGPA certificate, RHQ exemption mechanism) — [OpenOpps](https://openopps.com/blog/how-to-win-government-contracts-and-tenders-in-saudi-arabia-2026); [Gulf News](https://gulfnews.com/world/gulf/saudi/new-rule-saudi-arabia-reverses-restriction-allows-foreign-companies-in-government-projects-1.500451195)
- 77% expatriate employment — [Argaam](https://www.argaam.com/en/article/articledetail/id/1847820)
- Social-video saturation (TikTok 154% of adults, Snapchat 72% of population, WhatsApp ~90%+) — [Statista](https://statista.com/statistics/1299829/tiktok-penetration-worldwide-by-country); [DataReportal](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-saudi-arabia); [CST report via Minute Mirror](https://minutemirror.com.pk/whatsapp-is-most-used-app-in-saudi-arabia-report-finds-620330/)
- Media content standards and 2025 guidelines — [Al Tamimi](https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/the-sun-always-shines-on-tv-media-licensing-and-content-regulation-in-saudi-arabia); [Mimeta](https://www.mimeta.org/mimeta-news-on-censorship-in-art/2025/10/21/saudi-arabias-2025-media-rules-tighten-online-expression)
- Ramadan 6-hour cap — [Ajel](https://english.ajel.sa/news/saudi-arabia/mhrsd-announces-ramadan-working-hours-for-private-sector)
- ZATCA e-invoicing waves — [EY](https://globaltaxnews.ey.com/news/2026-1705-saudi-arabia-announces-25th-wave-of-phase-2-e-invoicing-integration)

### Inferences
Differences from a global playbook, in priority order:
1. The buying trigger is nationalisation compliance plus subsidy capture, not just engagement/retention; the product must produce HRDF/Tamkeen-compatible training evidence per national employee.
2. A free state platform (Doroob) already serves generic skills; positioning must be company-specific, job-specific, Arabic-dialect and format-led.
3. KSA-resident hosting and PDPL transfer safeguards are table stakes; government/semi-government tenants need absolute in-Kingdom data.
4. Government/PIF deals run through Etimad with a Local Content Certificate and (until the 2026 exemption) a regional HQ; a Saudi entity or licensed reseller is a structural need, not an optimisation.
5. Language stack inverts: Arabic (Gulf dialect) first, English second, then Urdu/Hindi/Bengali/Tagalog/Nepali for the expatriate majority (language mix inferred, not measured).
6. Content must be pre-screened against GCAM-style local content standards and conservative presentation norms; employee-generated public-style content is higher risk.
7. Engagement rails differ: WhatsApp and Snapchat idioms matter as much as TikTok; prayer-time and Ramadan scheduling must be built into delivery logic.
8. Commercials: SAR/AED pricing ex-VAT (15% KSA / 5% UAE), ZATCA-integrated Arabic invoices, and expectation of annual-prepay discounts (~15% in the one published example).
9. Social-video saturation is higher than in Western markets, so format adoption risk is lower while content-standards risk is higher.
10. Market is country-by-country: KSA is ~45% of GCC demand and the regulatory heaviest; UAE is fastest-growing and lighter on localisation outside regulated sectors; Qatar's 2025 law creates new obligations; Bahrain subsidises most generously; Kuwait's private sector employs very few nationals.

### Gaps
- Several items above rest on inference where primary data was unavailable (expatriate language mix, prayer-time engagement, GCAM applicability, discount norms); flagged in the relevant sections.
