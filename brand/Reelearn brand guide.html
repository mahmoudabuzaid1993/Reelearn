A minute a day. Remembered for months.دقيقة كل يوم، تبقى لشهور.

Reelearn feeds employees short vertical learning videos on the topics their employer chooses, in a feed that ends. This guide fixes the idea, the logo, the colours, the type, the voice and the first applications so that everything the company makes looks and sounds like one product.

01 · Brand idea

What the brand stands for, who it serves, and the single idea every decision is tested against.

Finite by design

Social feeds are built to never end. Reelearn borrows their format (vertical video, swipe, a hook in the first three seconds) and rejects their economics. Every session has a start, a recall question and a "done for today" screen. The brand promise is small on purpose: one minute a day that people still remember months later. That is also the only claim the learning evidence supports, which keeps the brand honest with the HR buyer who has to defend the purchase.

The L&D buyer

Needs to look rigorous in front of a CHRO and an HRDF auditor. Must feel: credible, measurable, safe to put the company name on.

The employee

A shop-floor associate, a branch teller, a nurse, often on a personal phone between tasks. Must feel: respected, finished quickly, a little proud.

The manager

Wants to see who is ready without chasing. Must feel: informed in one glance, never turned into an enforcer.

Personality

Brief

Says one thing per clip, one thing per sentence.

Warm

Talks like a good colleague, not a compliance notice.

Finite

Always tells you how much is left and when you are done.

Bilingual by birth

Arabic and English carry equal weight; expat languages are first-class, not a toggle.

Evidence-led

Shows recall scores, not watch time. Never brags about minutes spent.

Why the name works

"Reel" and "learn" share one letter. Reellearn collapses to Reelearn, so the brand owns the moment where watching turns into knowing. In Arabic, ريليرن places the lam (ل), the same letter, as the tallest stroke in the middle of the word. The logo is built on that shared letter.

02 · Logo

One device in three forms: the reel card that replaces the shared "l", the coral letter in running text, and the card on its own as the app mark.

The reel card is the letter

The card is a 300 × 740 rounded rectangle, the proportion of a vertical video frame stretched to the ascender height of Readex Pro. The check inside is drawn at the same stroke weight as the letters, so the card reads as a letter and not as a sticker. In the Arabic wordmark the lam is set in coral instead, because an Arabic word must stay joined at the baseline.

Rules

Clear space

Keep a margin equal to the card's width (the "l") on every side of any wordmark or lockup. Nothing else enters that space.

Minimum size

Display wordmark: 120 px or 30 mm wide. Below that, switch to the text wordmark. Mark alone: 24 px or 6 mm tall. App icon: supplied at 1024 px.

Colour

Ink on paper, white on night, coral card in both. Mono ink and mono white exist for print and engraving. No other colourways.

Bilingual order

Left-to-right surfaces: English then Arabic. Right-to-left surfaces (Arabic UI, Arabic print): Arabic then English. Never stack one language smaller than the other.

Do not

  • Put a play triangle, a phone outline or a film strip anywhere near the mark. The card already says "reel".
  • Tilt, outline, bevel, add gradients or drop shadows, or recolour the check.
  • Set the name in another typeface, in all caps, or as "ReeLearn" / "Reel Learn".
  • Use the display wordmark below 120 px wide; the check turns into noise.
  • Place the coral card on a coral background, or ink on night, where it disappears.
03 · Colour

A deep ink, a coral that belongs to the reel, and a mint that only ever means "correct". Everything else is paper.

Ink carries, coral points, mint rewards

Ink#0D2B3EText, wordmark, admin surfaces, app icon ground
Reel Coral#FF6257The card, the shared letter, primary buttons, progress
Recall Mint#2EC4B6Correct answers, "done", streak kept. Nothing else.
Paper#F6F7F5Light backgrounds, cards, documents
Night#081822Reel player, dark mode, reversed logo
Slate#5B6B78Secondary text, captions, "not yet" states

Proportion

Roughly 60 ink / 30 paper / 10 coral on any screen or page. Mint appears only as feedback, so it never shows in the layout before the person has done something. If a design uses mint decoratively, the feedback loses its meaning.

Semantic rule: no red cross

Reelearn never punishes a wrong answer with red. A missed question is shown in Slate with the right answer in Mint and a "see it again tomorrow" note. The brand's accent is warm, so red would also collide with it. Alerts that are not about learning (payment failed, upload rejected) use the admin console's standard amber #D9822B and critical #C0392B, never in the employee app.

Contrast

Ink on Paper 13.6:1 and white on Ink 12.4:1 pass every level. White on Reel Coral is 3.1:1, so coral buttons carry text at 16 px semibold or larger and coral is never used for body text. Ink on Mint is 8.3:1 for feedback chips.

05 · Voice

How Reelearn writes in notifications, captions, admin screens and sales material, in both languages.

Short, warm, and always says when you are done

Say

  • "Today's reels" / "مقاطع اليوم"
  • "Done for today. See you tomorrow." / "خلصت اليوم. نشوفك بكرة."
  • "You remembered 8 of 10." / "تذكرت ٨ من ١٠."
  • "One minute, then back to the floor."
  • "Your team is ready on AML: 91 %."
  • "Not yet. Here is the answer; it comes back Thursday."

Avoid

  • "Scroll", "binge", "addictive", "endless", "feed" in anything a buyer or employee reads
  • "TikTok for training" in the company's own words (journalists may say it; we do not)
  • "Mandatory", "overdue", "non-compliant" in the employee app
  • Streak shaming: "You lost your streak!" Use "Streak paused. Pick it up today."
  • Exclamation marks in Arabic or English beyond one per screen
  • Watch-time brags: "Employees spent 4,000 hours learning"

Three registers

Employee app · Gulf colloquial Arabic, plain English

Second person, present tense, contractions allowed. Arabic uses light white dialect ("نشوفك بكرة"), never classical. Urdu, Hindi, Bengali and Tagalog strings are translated from the Arabic, not the English, so the warmth survives.

Admin console · Modern Standard Arabic, neutral English

Nouns over verbs, numbers with units, no jokes. "Weekly active learners: 1,204 (78 %)". Arabic in MSA so it can be pasted into an HRDF submission.

Sales and HRDF material · evidence first

Lead with the recall score and the operational KPI, then the format. Cite the study when claiming anything about memory. Never claim "proven" without a control group.

08 · Files and next steps

What is in the logo folder and what has to happen before the identity is used in public.

Sixteen SVGs, all outlines

Every wordmark is converted to paths from Readex Pro 600, so no font is required to render them. Files: reelearn-wordmark (display, reversed, mono, text, text-plain), reelearn-wordmark-ar (default, reversed, plain), reelearn-lockup-bilingual, reelearn-lockup-stacked, reelearn-mark (coral, ink, white), reelearn-app-icon (primary, light), reelearn-avatar.

  • Register reelearn.sa, reelearn.app and reelearn.io; reelearn.com is held by a French productivity site since 2018.
  • File the wordmark and the card mark at SAIP in classes 9, 41 and 42 before launch; check EUIPO/USPTO for "Re:learn" and "REDeLEARN" before any expansion.
  • Readex Pro and IBM Plex Sans Arabic are under the SIL Open Font License: free for the app, the web and the logo outlines.
  • Test the display wordmark on a dark WhatsApp profile and a 48 px LinkedIn avatar before freezing the check weight.
  • Commission the Urdu, Hindi, Bengali and Tagalog UI strings from the Arabic master, and typeset them in Readex Pro's companion faces (Noto Sans for Devanagari and Bengali).
